A Rail402 facilitator settles `exact` payments in the tokens its operator accepts. By default that is Circle
USDC on each network. Any other SEP-41 token can be added: a classic asset through its Stellar Asset
Contract, or a custom token contract, with any number of decimals the configuration accepts (0 to 38). The
tests below cover 6, 7 and 18 decimals.

## Accepting another SEP-41 token

List every accepted token in `ASSETS` for the network, as `CONTRACT:SYMBOL:DECIMALS[:MIN:MAX]`, comma-separated.
Listing assets replaces the default, so keep USDC in the list if you still accept it:

```sh
TESTNET_ASSETS=CBIELTK6YBZJU5UP2WWQEUCYKLPU6AUNZ2BQ4WWFEIE3USCIHMXQDAMA:USDC:7,CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK:TEST6:6
```

- `CONTRACT` is the token's `C…` address.
- `SYMBOL` labels the token in metrics, `/health` and search filters (`asset=TEST6`).
- `DECIMALS` must equal the token's own `decimals()`. It never changes an amount: x402 amounts are always base
  units, and the facilitator compares them exactly. It turns a search's `maxPrice` in whole units into base
  units.
- `MIN` and `MAX` bound the accepted amount in base units. Amounts up to the `i128` maximum are accepted, so
  an 18-decimal token's amounts beyond the 64-bit range settle like any other.

## What the service checks at startup

Before it provisions channels, the service reads every configured token from the network: whether its
contract instance exists, whether it is a Stellar Asset Contract or another contract, and its `decimals()`.

| Finding                                                                                    | Result                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The token's `decimals()` differs from `DECIMALS`                                           | The service stops with exit code 78: `rail402: asset_decimals_mismatch: TESTNET_ASSETS: TEST6 (C…) has 6 decimals on stellar:testnet, but 7 are configured` |
| The contract has no `decimals()` function, or answers it with something other than a `u32` | The service stops with exit code 78 and `asset_not_a_token`                                                                                                 |
| The contract is not deployed                                                               | The service starts; `/ready` fails with `stellar:testnet:assets` naming the contract                                                                        |
| The RPC does not answer the reads, or `decimals()` fails in any other way                  | The service starts; `/ready` fails with `stellar:testnet:assets` naming the read that failed, and the read is retried                                       |
| Every token matches                                                                        | `stellar:testnet:assets` passes with the tokens and their decimals, e.g. `USDC:7, TEST6:6`                                                                  |

Until the check passes, the background poll (every 15 seconds) reads the tokens again, and `/ready` passes
once they match. A mismatch found then keeps `/ready` failing and is logged as an error; fix the
configuration and restart. Every accepted token's contract TTL is watched too (see
[Operations](/operations)).

## Classic assets and custom tokens

Both kinds settle the same way: one `transfer(from, to, amount)` signed by the payer, submitted by the
facilitator, which only pays the fee. They differ in what a payment needs and in how a failure is named.

|                                         | Stellar Asset Contract (e.g. USDC)                                          | Custom SEP-41 token (e.g. TEST6)                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Holding the token                       | A `G…` account needs a trustline; a `C…` account does not                   | No trustline; any account can hold it                                                                     |
| A payer holding less than the amount    | `insufficient_funds`                                                        | `insufficient_funds`, confirmed by reading the payer's `balance()`                                        |
| Other token errors                      | Named from the contract's error number (trustline missing, not authorized…) | `invalid_exact_stellar_payload_token_rejected`: "The token contract rejected the transfer with error #N." |
| Bazaar listing: can `payTo` receive it? | Decided from the trustline                                                  | Left unknown for a `G…` account; always yes for a `C…` account                                            |

The rules are listed with the other failure causes in
[Errors and verification rules](/verification-rules#7-why-a-simulation-failed).

### Fees and the fee ceiling

A custom token runs its own WASM and keeps its own storage, so a payment in it can cost far more network fee
than one in a Stellar Asset Contract, mostly as rent for the token's storage entries. The test token, built on
OpenZeppelin's fungible token, extends a holder's balance entry to 30 days whenever it reads one with less than
29 days left, and the payment whose transfer reads it pays that rent. Measured on `stellar:testnet` in a
rehearsal of the [canonical run](/reference/conformance) (`--asset usdc,test6 --payer g,c`) against a Rail402
facilitator; the recorded canonical run lists the fee charged for each of its transactions, which varies with
network conditions:

| Payment                                               | Fee charged (stroops) |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| USDC, `G…` payer                                      | 23,131                |
| USDC, `C…` payer                                      | 20,327                |
| TEST6, `G…` payer, the payee's first TEST6            | 315,702               |
| TEST6, `C…` payer, to a payee that already held TEST6 | 463,067               |

The fee a simulation asks for is higher than the fee finally charged, because it includes refundable rent:
361,384 stroops for a smart-account payer's first TEST6 payment to a new holder, and 539,505 for one to a
payee that already held TEST6.

The facilitator compares the fee the simulation asks for with `MAX_TX_FEE_STROOPS` and refuses a payment
above it with `invalid_exact_stellar_payload_fee_exceeds_maximum`, before anything is submitted. The default
is 1,000,000 stroops on testnet, which covers every TEST6 payment above, and 300,000 on pubnet. When you accept
a custom token on pubnet, set the ceiling for that token's costliest payments. `upto` settlements have their
own ceiling, `UPTO_MAX_TX_FEE_STROOPS` (default 5,000,000 on testnet, 500,000 on pubnet): a smart-account
payer's `upto` payment in TEST6 to a payee that already held TEST6 was refused under a 500,000 ceiling and
settled with 1,000,000. See [fee ceilings](/configuration#fee-ceilings) for the measured costs behind the
defaults.
On a local network with default settings the same test-token payments cost about 160,000 stroops.

## Pricing in a token

A stock x402 seller prices a resource in a custom token with an asset amount in base units, for example 0.25
TEST6 as `{ amount: "250000", asset: "CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK" }`. The
`exact()` helper of `@rail402.dev/sdk/seller` converts a price in whole units with the token's decimals, which
`assetDecimals()` reads from the network:

```ts
import { assetDecimals, exact } from "@rail402.dev/sdk/seller";

const asset = "CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK";
const decimals = await assetDecimals(asset, {
  network: "stellar:testnet",
  rpcUrl: "https://soroban-testnet.stellar.org",
});
const option = exact({
  network: "stellar:testnet",
  payTo: "G...",
  price: { amount: "0.25", asset, decimals },
});
// option.price is { amount: "250000", asset }: 0.25 with 6 decimals
```

The same 0.25 is `2500000` base units of a 7-decimal token and `250000000000000000` of an 18-decimal one.

## The TEST6 test token

`TEST6` is a SEP-41 test token with 6 decimals on `stellar:testnet`, at
[`CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK`](https://stellar.expert/explorer/testnet/contract/CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK).
It has no value, no admin and an open faucet: anyone can mint 100 `TEST6` to any address.

```sh
stellar contract invoke --id CDS45IRSWVDBV4JM2FPZ52EYXYO54J6DJL35GOSXOTRA4BYM6FFRK2BK --network testnet \
  --source <your account> -- faucet --to <address>
```

Its source is `contracts/testing/test-token` in the repository, and its deployment record, with the
transactions and the WASM hash, is `contracts/testing/deployments/test6-testnet.json`.

## How it is tested

`tools/conformance/test/assets.integration.test.ts` runs the service as a process on a local network with
three accepted tokens: a classic asset's Stellar Asset Contract (7 decimals), the test token deployed with 6
decimals (`TEST6`) and with 18 (`TEST18`). For each token, a stock `@x402/express` seller and a stock
`@x402/fetch` buyer complete payments from a `G…` payer and from a `C…` smart-account payer, and to a `C…`
`payTo`; `TEST18`'s price of 12.5 is 12,500,000,000,000,000,000 base units, beyond the 64-bit range. Each
payment is read back from the chain:

- exactly one `transfer` event, from the payer to `payTo`, for the price, from the token's contract;
- the payer's balance falls and `payTo`'s rises by the price, and no facilitator account holds or moves the
  token;
- the fee is paid by the sponsor, through a fee bump around a channel account, and the sponsor's XLM falls by
  exactly the fee charged.

The same three tokens are paid with `upto`: a stock seller with `@rail402.dev/upto`'s server scheme prices
the resource at the cap and charges 40% of it through the stock settlement override, the buyer pays with the
scheme's own client, and only the charged amount moves, from payer to `payTo`, while the Bazaar lists the
resource at its cap.

The same suite checks the Bazaar listing's token facts (`symbol`, `decimals`, `receivable`), search by
`asset=<symbol>` and a `maxPrice` in whole units, `insufficient_funds` for a payer holding less than the
amount and for one holding none, and the startup checks above.
