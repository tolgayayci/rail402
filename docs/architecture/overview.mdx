title: Architecture overview
description: The x402 payment loop on Stellar, where Rail402 sits in it, and the components it is built from.
---

x402 has three parties. The **buyer** asks for a resource, the **seller** (the resource server) names a
price, and the **facilitator** checks the payment and puts it on-chain. Rail402 is the facilitator. Buyers
and sellers use the stock x402 packages and never talk to Stellar directly about fees. Rail402 settles two
schemes: `exact`, a fixed price, and `upto`, a cap the buyer signs of which the seller settles what the call
used.

## The exact payment loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="The seller asks for payment">
    The buyer requests a paid route. The seller answers `402 Payment Required` with a `PAYMENT-REQUIRED`
    header that lists what it accepts: scheme `exact`, network `stellar:testnet`, the asset's contract
    address, the `payTo` address, the amount in the asset's base units, `maxTimeoutSeconds`, and
    `extra.areFeesSponsored: true`, which the seller's scheme reads from Rail402's `/supported`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The buyer signs an authorization">
    The buyer's x402 client builds a Soroban invocation of the asset contract's `transfer(from, to, amount)`
    and signs its authorization entry with the buyer's key. The signature fixes the asset, the recipient and
    the amount. The client sends the payment as `payload: {transaction}` in the `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The seller calls /verify">
    Rail402 runs its own checks first, before any simulation: protocol, operator policy, transaction shape,
    accounts, authorization and expiry. It then runs the unmodified `@x402/stellar` verifier, which simulates
    the transaction against the current ledger. When simulation fails, Rail402 names the on-chain cause, for
    example a missing trustline or a spent nonce.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The seller calls /settle">
    Rail402 runs its own checks again, claims the payer's authorization and leases one of its channel
    accounts. Upstream `@x402/stellar` then runs its verifier again and rebuilds the transaction around the
    buyer's signed authorization with the channel account as the source, and Rail402's sponsor account wraps
    it in a fee bump and pays the fee. Rail402 records the signed envelope before broadcasting it, then waits
    for the ledger.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The seller serves the resource">
    The settle response carries the transaction hash. The seller returns it to the buyer in the
    `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` header together with the resource.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The upto payment loop

`upto` follows the same loop with three differences; [upto payments](/facilitator/upto) has the details.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The seller names a cap and a settlement authority">
    The `upto` option's `amount` is the cap, and `extra.facilitatorAddress` names the facilitator's settlement
    authority, which the seller's scheme copies from `/supported`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The buyer signs terms, not a transfer">
    The buyer signs one authorization entry for `settle(terms)` on the `upto` settlement contract: the
    recipient, token, cap, settlement authority and an expiration ledger, with one nested
    `token.approve(payer, settlement contract, cap, expiration)`. `/verify` simulates the settlement for the
    full cap.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The seller settles what it used">
    After serving, the seller names the amount, from zero to the cap, and calls `/settle`. Rail402 submits
    `settle(payer, terms, amount)` with the settlement authority as the operation source; the contract checks
    the buyer's terms, the authority's authorization and `0 ≤ amount ≤ cap`, and pulls exactly the amount from
    the buyer to `payTo`. The sponsor pays the fee through a fee bump, as for `exact`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The contract is immutable, has no admin and stores nothing; single use comes from the payer's authorization
nonce. A TTL keeper in the service keeps the contract's instance and code entries live on the network, and
`/health` reports their remaining TTL ([upto contract](/reference/upto-contract)).

## What the facilitator can and cannot do

Rail402 is non-custodial. It holds one secret per network, the **sponsor** seed, and derives two kinds of
account from it: its **channel accounts** and, when `upto` is served, one **settlement authority**.

- The sponsor pays every settlement fee and the base reserves of the channel accounts and the authority. It
  needs only XLM.
- Channel accounts are the source accounts of settlement transactions, one settlement at a time each, so
  concurrent settlements never compete for a sequence number. They hold no balance.
- The settlement authority holds no funds either. It is the operation source of `upto` settlements, so it
  chooses the settled amount between zero and the cap the buyer signed, or declines to settle; it cannot
  exceed the cap, change the recipient or token, reuse an authorization or move anyone's funds.
- Verification refuses any payment in which a facilitator account (the sponsor, a channel account or the
  settlement authority) is the payer or a party to one of its authorizations, and an `exact` payment whose
  transaction or operation source is one; an `upto` payment's `payTo` may not be a facilitator account either.
- For `exact`, the payer's signature covers the asset, recipient and amount. For `upto`, it covers the
  asset, recipient, cap, settlement authority and expiration. Changing any of them after signing fails
  signature verification.

The facilitator can therefore only spend its own XLM on fees. See [Operations](/operations) for key custody
and the limits that protect the sponsor.

## Settlement is idempotent and crash-safe

Each settlement is keyed by the payer's authorization (network, payer, nonce).

- A repeated `/settle` for the same payment returns the recorded result, and funds move once. The
  ledger is consulted first, so this holds even after the authorization expired or while the sponsor
  guard refuses new settlements. The repeat is marked for the seller in `EXTENSION-RESPONSES`.
- A different request for an authorization already held (another envelope, recipient, asset or
  amount) is refused (`settle_exact_stellar_idempotency_conflict`, or `settle_upto_stellar_idempotency_conflict`
  for an `upto` settlement asked for another amount).
- If the transaction is not final within the confirmation window, `/settle` answers `settlement_pending`
  with the transaction hash, and a background reconciler finishes it from the recorded bytes.
- After a crash or restart, only the recorded bytes are ever resubmitted, so there is never a second
  transaction for the same authorization.

The full list of checks, codes and tests is in [Errors and verification rules](/verification-rules).

## The Bazaar

A payment can carry the x402 `bazaar` extension, which describes the paid resource: its input and output
schema, method or tool name, and service metadata. When a payment with that extension settles, Rail402 adds
the resource to its catalog, provided the metadata is valid and, for an `http` or `https` resource URL, the
host is public. A payment without the extension is settled normally and not cataloged.

- **Settlement-gated.** Nothing is listed without a settled payment to it. `/verify` only previews the
  outcome.
- **Bound to the payee.** The listing belongs to the `payTo` the settlement paid. Another seller cannot
  change it.
- **Confirmed at the origin.** An HTTP resource is listed, and any change to it published, only after the
  resource's own `402` response confirms it.
- **Off the payment path.** Cataloging never changes the payment result and adds no transaction. The seller
  learns the outcome from the `EXTENSION-RESPONSES` header of the facilitator's response.
- **`upto` listings.** An `upto` resource is listed at its cap, the most a call can cost. A settlement of
  zero, on chain or off, never creates or changes a listing: a listing needs a settlement that moved value
  to its `payTo` ([cataloging rules](/bazaar/cataloging)).

Buyers and agents read the catalog through [`GET /discovery/resources`](/bazaar/discovery) and
[`GET /discovery/search`](/bazaar/search). The catalog lives in Postgres, not on-chain.

## Networks

Rail402 knows the CAIP-2 networks `stellar:testnet` and `stellar:pubnet`. Which ones an instance serves is
configuration (`NETWORKS`). The [hosted instance](/facilitator/hosted-testnet) serves `stellar:testnet`
only. The tests and conformance runs exercise testnet. Pubnet can be configured, with stricter rules that
the service checks at startup (see [Configuration](/configuration)), but it has not been exercised end to
end.

## Components

Rail402 is one service backed by Postgres, built from workspace packages that can also be used on their own.

| Component                 | Role                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `apps/rail402`            | The HTTP service: `/supported`, `/verify`, `/settle`, `/discovery/*`, health, metrics and metering |
| `packages/facilitator`    | The settlement engine on `@x402/stellar`: preflight checks, fee sponsorship, crash-safe settlement |
| `packages/upto`           | The `upto` scheme for Stellar: its client, resource server and facilitator rules                   |
| `contracts/stellar/upto`  | The `upto` settlement contract that enforces the cap on chain                                      |
| `packages/stellar`        | Stellar payload inspection, reason codes and diagnosis of on-chain failures                        |
| `packages/bazaar`         | The catalog: cataloging from settlements, integrity rules, origin checks and version history       |
| `packages/search`         | Natural-language search: BM25F, local embeddings and reciprocal rank fusion                        |
| `packages/store-postgres` | Durable settlement ledger, channel-account leases, catalog, rate limits and metering               |

Without Postgres (`STORE=memory`) the same service runs in one process with no durable state, which suits
local development and tests. See [Configuration](/configuration).

The service also runs background tasks: the reconciler that finishes unconfirmed settlements, the network
poll behind `/health` and `/ready`, the `upto` settlement authority's provisioning, the contract TTL monitor
and keeper, and the Bazaar's origin checks and search indexing.

Buyers and agents can use the stock x402 clients, or the agent tools built on them: the buyer SDK
(`packages/sdk`), the MCP server (`packages/mcp`) and the CLI (`packages/cli`). They discover resources
through the Bazaar, pay under a spending policy, and reach sellers through `packages/safe-fetch`, which
refuses private addresses and pins DNS ([Agents](/agents/sdk)).
