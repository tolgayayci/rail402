---
title: "FAQ"
description: "Common questions about paying, selling, and running services with Rail402 on Stellar."
---

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What network does Rail402 run on?">
    `stellar:testnet`, live at `https://facilitator.rail402.dev`, free and with no API key. `/supported` is the ground truth for what any facilitator serves.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need Rail402-specific packages to use it?">
    No. The facilitator and Bazaar speak the x402 v2 wire format, so a stock `@x402` client pays a Rail402 endpoint and reads the Rail402 Bazaar with no Rail402-specific code. The `@rail402.dev/*` helper packages add convenience such as spend caps and preflight checks. They live in the Rail402 repository, not on npm, and you never need them to interoperate.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does a buyer need XLM?">
    No. The facilitator sponsors the network fee, so a buyer holds only the payment asset. `/supported` advertises this as `extra.areFeesSponsored: true`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which assets can I pay in?">
    Any SEP-41 token, with USDC as the default. The testnet USDC contract is `CBIELTK6YBZJU5UP2WWQEUCYKLPU6AUNZ2BQ4WWFEIE3USCIHMXQDAMA`. Both the buyer and the seller need a trustline to the asset.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How does my service get listed in the Bazaar?">
    Automatically, when its first payment settles. You add discovery metadata to your paywall with one `declareDiscoveryExtension` call, and the facilitator catalogs the resource at settlement. There is no registration step and no dashboard. See the [Seller quickstart](/seller/quickstart).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between exact and upto?">
    `exact` charges a fixed price to a fixed recipient. `upto` authorizes a ceiling and settles the actual usage, which fits metered services such as token billing. The unused difference never leaves the buyer's wallet. See [Payment schemes](/reference/schemes).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is the facilitator custodial?">
    No. The facilitator never holds funds and is never the source of funds. It submits the buyer-signed authorization exactly as signed. On chain, the transfer sender is the buyer and the fee is charged to the facilitator. Tampering with the amount, recipient, or asset fails signature verification.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I get testnet funds?">
    Generate and fund an account with friendbot in [Stellar Lab](https://lab.stellar.org/account/create), add a trustline to testnet USDC, and get USDC from the [Circle faucet](https://faucet.circle.com/). Buyers need the asset; sellers need only the trustline.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I run my own facilitator?">
    Yes. It ships as a Docker image and runs on `stellar:testnet` free and open by default. You can also run verify and settle in-process, with no external facilitator, when you sell your own services. See the [Operator quickstart](/operator/quickstart).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a smart-contract account pay?">
    Yes. The facilitator settles payments from `__check_auth` smart-contract (`C...`) accounts, and the `oz-account` canary proves both `exact` and `upto` from one on testnet, including an over-budget payment refused by the account's own policy. The buyer-side helper packages currently sign with a classic keypair, so paying from a smart account through them is not yet wired.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I confirm a payment settled?">
    Take the transaction hash from the `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` header or the settle response, and open it on [stellar.expert](https://stellar.expert/explorer/testnet) or the [Explorer](/explorer/overview). The Explorer classifies the payment and shows the fee sponsorship read from the ledger.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What license is Rail402 under?">
    Apache-2.0, a permissive OSI-approved license, so you can host it, fork it, or self-facilitate without depending on a single operator.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
